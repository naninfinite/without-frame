## Set out to

Compare the current AI models for the multi-agent setup, then work out whether a 3D tactics game in Godot could run on the HackberryPi CM5.

## What happened

- Compared Claude/Claude Code, ChatGPT/Codex, Gemini/Antigravity, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4.1 and GLM-5.3 for strengths, weaknesses and how to mix them. Key update: Opus and Sonnet 5.5 are now faster than Codex, and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash added vision.
- Settled on an SRPG in the vein of FFT, FFTA, FFTA2 and Fire Emblem, built in Godot for the Hackberry.
- Confirmed the Hackberry's weak point is the GPU, not the 16GB RAM. The Mobile renderer and a 360×360 render scaled 2× make sprites on 3D terrain realistic.
- Worked through FFT's charge-time system in detail, and compared it with ATB in a timeline of the same Fire-dodge scenario.
- Designed how the project runs: docs as the source of truth, decision records, lanes, shift changes, cards and firing, a parking lot. Then cut the team list from fifteen down to four to start.
- Scaffolded the repo.

## Decisions

- [ADR-001](../docs/decisions/ADR-001-sprites-on-3d-terrain.md): FFT-style sprites on 3D terrain. Rotation is a must.
- [ADR-002](../docs/decisions/ADR-002-fft-charge-time.md): FFT charge time.
- [ADR-003](../docs/decisions/ADR-003-atb-parked.md): ATB parked, not adopted.
- [ADR-004](../docs/decisions/ADR-004-rules-view-split.md): battle rules separate from visuals.
- [ADR-005](../docs/decisions/ADR-005-four-starting-lanes.md): start with four lanes.
- [ADR-006](../docs/decisions/ADR-006-laws-with-teeth.md): FFTA laws with real punishments, the way FFTA had them before FFTA2 softened them.

## Surprises and lessons

- FFT's charge time is essentially ATB with the clock paused, and the same designer (Hiroyuki Ito) created both.
- FFT had camera rotation because its maps were real 3D. FFTA, on the GBA, was pure 2D.
- The biggest risk isn't technical. It's spending weeks on process before anything moves on screen. Hence two spikes first.
